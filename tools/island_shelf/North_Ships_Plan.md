# North Ships Plan (draft for corrections)
14 ordinary ships (7 tiny, 5 small, 2 medium) plus 2 ghost ships. 43 ordinary crew plus 9 ghosts = 52 people. The guild sends 4 at most, so no ship is bigger than medium. Most ships are harmless; a few are lonely and clingy; a few carry a threat or want something intimate. Crews are mostly human with a few frost-folk from Coldwatch. Looks: cold-weathered, oilskins and wool, red noses, frost in beards.

## Tiny ships (2 crew each)
1. The Ice Tug: a tug hauling harbour ice. Man 50 and woman 44, a bickering married pair. Harmless; trade ice and gossip.
2. The Tidewick Ferry Skiff: two women 38 and 29, cheerful fishers. Harmless; sell fish and swap news.
3. The Sealers' Dinghy: two brothers 41 and 36, gruff. Harmless; ask for tobacco and tell tall tales.
4. The Coal Lighter: man 55 and woman 33, coal-black with soot. Harmless; sell coal and gossip.
5. The Lamp-Tender: an old lighthouse-tender man 62 and his assistant, a woman 27. Lonely and grabby: they keep you talking, then keep trying to keep you aboard; the woman is touchy and forward.
6. The Smugglers' Skiff: two men 34 and 40, shifty. A threat: they size up a small party and rob it if it looks weak; will bargain if it looks strong.
7. The Pleasure Sledge-Boat: a bored rich woman 45 and her boatman 31. She wants an intimate evening with one of the party and pays well; he is quietly resentful. Suggestive, nothing forced.

## Small ships (3 to 4 crew)
8. The Herring Drifter: 4 crew, mixed (men 47 and 24, women 39 and 30). Harmless; share the catch and trade gear.
9. The Whalers' Chaser: 4 men 28 to 49, loud and drunk. Rowdy; pick a fight if you stare, then buy you a drink.
10. The Fog Trader: 3 crew, mixed (woman 52, man 36, woman 25). A floating shop of odds and ends; fair prices, sharp haggling.
11. The Pilot Cutter: 3 women 58, 34 and 26, expert pilots. Harmless and useful; sell charts and weather warnings.
12. The Wreckers' Lugger: 4 crew, mixed (men 45 and 38, women 33 and 29). A real threat: false lights to lure ships onto the rocks, then strip them; they will try the same on you at night.

## Medium ships (5 to 6 crew)
13. The Bone Ship (whaler): 6 crew (captain man 56, mate woman 41, two hands men 35 and 29, cook woman 49, deckhand man 22). Cruel captain; demands a "harbour fee" from smaller vessels and bullies a weak party; the cook is kind.
14. The Expedition Ship: 5 crew, scholars and a mate (woman 48, men 55 and 31, woman 27, man 38). Harmless and curious; want to see the party's gear and will pay for stories.

## Ghost ships (North; haunting ground Coldwatch, Wrackmoor, Gloamfjord)
15. The Downed Military Ship (medium, 6): captain man 51, lieutenant woman 34, sergeant man 44, surgeon woman 39, two sailors men 25 and 28. Stuck on their last order; forceful and pushy, they demand papers and try to commandeer the party for their mission, and they back down to respect.
16. The Lost Sealers (small, 3): men 40, 33 and 27, died in a whiteout. Polite and confused; ask the way to a harbour that is not there any more.

## Rule: interactions only
Encounters are meetings at sea only (talk, trade, a threat, a favour asked, a challenge). No carrying passengers, no hiring, no taking messages or jobs, because the game cannot log any of that at sea.

## Running order
Tiny, small and medium ships roll by distance from the island group; ghost ships join the roll only while heading toward the haunting ground and weigh more the closer the party gets.

## Questions with defaults
1. Is the share right: about 3 trouble ships (6, 12, 13), 3 clingy or suggestive (5, 7, 9), 8 harmless, 2 ghosts? Default yes.
2. Frost-folk crew: should any ship be frost-folk? Default one tiny ship (the coal lighter, ship 4, becomes frost-folk), the rest human.
3. Do you want ship names now? Default: I give each a distinct working name when I build.
